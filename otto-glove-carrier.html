Otto glove carrier
Rev A

A custom PCB to organize the glove’s contact inputs, external microphone connection and battery interface into a compact, serviceable wearable assembly.

The design problem

The wearable interface needs connections for fingertip contacts, a microphone and a battery, while keeping the controller removable. I designed a separate carrier rather than putting a bare ESP32 chip onto a new board.

The existing XIAO ESP32-S3 remains the controller and connects to the carrier through short internal wires. The microphone is a separate breakout. The glove, microphone and battery connectors are arranged along the same board edge for the planned cuff enclosure.

My design work

I completed the schematic, assigned connector and component footprints, created custom wire-pad and fuse footprints, placed components and routed the board across two copper layers. I added ground pours and stitching vias, corrected silkscreen issues, and reviewed fabrication layers and drill outputs.

The carrier design includes four contact-input channels, a four-channel ESD-protection circuit, an external microphone interface, a fused and switched battery path, a battery-voltage divider and accessible test points. These are implemented design features; their physical behavior still needs first-board validation.

The fabrication handoff consisted of seven Gerber files and separate plated/non-plated Excellon drill files, submitted to PCBWay as a bare-board package.

Design-rule checkpoint

The final recorded KiCad DRC result showed zero active errors, zero active warnings and zero unconnected items. Three individual exclusions were documented for generic footprint-name filters on the intentional custom wire-pad footprints. No entire DRC test category was globally ignored.

What this result establishes
The completed design was checked against the configured rules and schematic. It is not evidence of electrical operation, correct physical battery polarity, enclosure fit, runtime or wearable safety.

Where it fits in Otto Mobile

The earlier working gesture interface used double thumb/index contact to arm, a thumb/middle hold to capture and release to submit. BLE carried the gesture commands to Otto Mobile, while a Pi-connected ReSpeaker handled microphone capture and enrolled earbuds handled playback.

This carrier is a later hardware revision. Its new cuff-microphone path and board assembly are not being represented as part of that already demonstrated voice loop. Keeping those checkpoints separate prevents a successful earlier prototype from overstating the new revision’s readiness.

Current status & next validation

The CAD and fabrication-file work is complete, and I am awaiting the boards after submitting them to PCBWay. Assembly, populated-board operation, microphone capture through this carrier, final enclosure fit and runtime/thermal testing remain pending.

  1. Inventory and first assembly
    Reconcile exact-fit components and assemble one carrier before populating the remaining boards.
  2. Electrical and functional checks
    Document inspection, continuity and supply checks, then test contact inputs, microphone capture and battery-voltage sensing.
  3. System and mechanical integration
    Validate firmware behavior, disconnect/reconnect handling, power behavior and enclosure fit before the finished wearable trial.

This case study is a portfolio summary, not a complete assembly procedure or a manufacturing release. The original native KiCad project remains the design master.
